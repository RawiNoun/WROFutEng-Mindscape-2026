# Components

This document lists every electromechanical component used on our WRO 2026 Future Engineers vehicle, what it does, and why we chose it. Each entry links to a photo stored in this same folder. For how these components are wired together, see `../hardware/cad-files/wiring-files/wiring.md`.

---

## Raspberry Pi 5 (8GB)

![Raspberry Pi 5 8GB](<RPI5_8gb.png>)

- **Role:** Main single-board computer (SBC) for the vehicle. Runs the control program: reads the camera and ultrasonic sensors, decides steering/driving commands, and sends them to the servo and the L298N motor driver.
- **Why chosen:** CPU power to run our camera-based wall-percentage detection and traffic-sign recognition in real time, while still fitting the vehicle's power budget on the 5V powerbank.
- **Powered from:** the 5V powerbank (logic side of the circuit), not the 12V motor battery — see the wiring documentation for why these are kept separate.

---

## Raspberry Pi Camera v2 (8MP)

![Raspberry Pi Camera v2 8MP](<rpi_cam_v2_8_mp.png>)

- **Role:** Primary sensing element for navigation. The image feed is processed to estimate what percentage of the frame is taken up by the left wall versus the right wall; the difference between the two is used as the main steering error signal to keep the robot centred between the walls. It is also used to detect and classify the red/green traffic-sign pillars in the Obstacle Challenge.
- **Interface:** connects directly to the Raspberry Pi 5's CSI camera port (CAM/DSP1) via ribbon cable — not through GPIO.
- **Why chosen:** 8MP resolution reliably estimates wall coverage and pillar colour/side at the frame rates we need, without producing more data than the Pi 5 needs to process per frame.

## Servo Motor — SG90

![Servo motor SG90](<SERVO(SG90).png>)

- **Role:** Steering actuator. Turns the front axle left/right based on the PWM signal generated by the Raspberry Pi (GPIO12/PWM0).
- **Why chosen:** small, light, and low current draw (fits comfortably within the powerbank's power budget), while providing enough torque and precision to steer the front wheels at competition speeds.
- **Powered from:** the 5V powerbank (logic side), directly from the Raspberry Pi's 5V/GND pins.

---

## DC Motor (yellow plastic gearbox motor)

![DC motor](<DC_motor.png>)

- **Role:** Driving motor. Connected through a gearbox to the rear axle, providing forward/backward motion. This is the vehicle's single driving motor, connected directly to the axle through the gearbox (not one motor per wheel), which satisfies the WRO requirement that driving wheels must be mechanically/physically connected (rules 11.3 / 11.5 / 11.13).
- **Controlled via:** the L298N motor driver's OUT1/OUT2 terminals, which set both direction and speed (through PWM on the enable pin).
- **Powered from:** the 4× 18650 Li-ion battery pack, through the L298N — kept off the 5V powerbank entirely to avoid motor current/noise affecting the Raspberry Pi.

---

## Motor Driver — L298N

![L298N motor driver](<Driver_l298n.png>)

- **Role:** H-bridge motor driver. Takes low-power logic signals from the Raspberry Pi (IN1–IN4, ENA/ENB) and uses them to switch the high-current 12V supply to the DC drive motor, allowing the Pi to control direction and speed without passing motor current through its own GPIO pins.
- **Why chosen:** simple, well-documented, widely available driver capable of handling our motor's current draw from the 12V battery, with enough logic inputs to control both direction and PWM speed from GPIO alone.

---

## Ultrasonic Sensors — HY-SRF05 (×2)

![HY-SRF05 ultrasonic sensor](<ULTRA_SONIC(HY-SRF05).png>)

- **Role:** Emergency braking safety layer — **not** used for continuous wall-distance measurement or wall following (that is done by the camera). Two HY-SRF05 sensors are mounted at different points on the vehicle and polled continuously in parallel with the main camera-based control loop. If either sensor reports a distance below a fixed safety threshold, the program immediately cuts/reverses the drive motor via the L298N regardless of what the camera-based logic is currently commanding, to prevent a hard collision with a wall or pillar.
- **Connections used:** VCC, GND, Trig (output from Pi), Echo (input to Pi) per sensor — see the wiring documentation for the exact GPIO pins.
- **Why chosen:** the HY-SRF05 is inexpensive, low current draw (fits the powerbank budget), and fast enough to act as a reliable last-resort safety check independent of camera lighting conditions.

## Battery Pack — 4× 18650 Li-ion Cells + 4-Cell Battery Box

![4-Cell Battery Box](<battery_pack.png>)
![4× 18650 Li-ion Cell](<rechargable_battery.png>)

- **Role:** Motor-side power source. Four rechargeable 3.7V 18650 Li-ion cells sit in a 4-cell holder/box, wired in series to feed the L298N's motor supply input (Vs), which in turn drives the DC drive motor.
- **Nominal voltage:** 4 × 3.7V ≈ 14.8V nominal (fully charged ~16.8V, cut-off ~12V) — this is the pack referred to as the "12V Battery" in the wiring diagram/schematic.
- **Why chosen:** rechargeable 18650 cells give a high-current, lightweight, and reusable power source for the drive motor without needing disposable batteries, and are kept completely isolated from the Raspberry Pi's 5V logic supply to avoid motor noise/voltage sag affecting the electronics.

---

## Powerbank — Joyroom 10000 mAh (Fast Charging, Digital Display)

![Joyroom 10000 mAh (Fast Charging, Digital Display)](<powerbank.png>)

- **Role:** 5V logic-side power source for the whole electronics stack: Raspberry Pi 5, camera, both ultrasonic sensors, the servo, and the logic side of the L298N. Rated at a maximum output of 22.5 W, which we treat as a hard design constraint (see `wiring.md` for the power-budget reasoning).
- **Why chosen:** 10000 mAh gives enough runtime for a full competition day of practice + rounds, the digital display lets us monitor remaining charge between rounds, and fast-charging support means it can be topped up quickly during breaks.

---

## 5V Cooling Fan

![Cooling Fan](<cooling_fan.png>)

- **Role:** Active cooling for the Raspberry Pi 5 (and/or the L298N driver, depending on final mounting), preventing thermal throttling during extended runs, which would otherwise slow down our camera-processing frame rate mid-round.
- **Powered from:** the 5V powerbank, alongside the rest of the logic-side electronics.
- **Why chosen:** the Raspberry Pi 5 runs noticeably hotter than previous Pi models under sustained camera-processing load; active cooling keeps performance consistent across a full 3-minute round and back-to-back rounds during the competition day.

---

## Status LED (Code-Running Indicator)

![Status LED](<led.png>)

- **Role:** Paired with current-limiting resistor R1, this LED is driven by a GPIO output and lights up specifically to indicate that **the control program is running** — not simply that the vehicle is powered on. This gives judges and team members an immediate visual check, from outside the vehicle, of whether the program has actually started after the push button is pressed.
- **Why chosen:** a simple, low-current visual indicator that requires only one GPIO pin and a resistor, with no impact on the power budget, while making the vehicle's state easy to verify during checks and rounds.

---

## Mini Breadboard

![Mini breadboard](<mini_breadboard.png>)

- **Role:** Used to prototype and hold the small supporting circuit — the status LED (D1/R1, described above) and the push-button (SW1) used as the mandatory WRO **Start button** — before/instead of a soldered board.
- **Why chosen:** allows quick iteration on the LED/button circuit during development without committing to solder, while keeping the wiring simple enough to be reliable in competition.
